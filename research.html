A Recursive Prime Decomposition Conjecture (C2)

Zenodo · 10.5281/zenodo.20225703

Conjecture. For every prime $p>2$ there are $q, r \in \{1\}\cup\mathbb{P}$ with $q<p$ such that $p = 2q + r$ or $p = 2q - r$. Since $r$ decomposes too, every prime unfolds into a binary tree whose leaves are all $1$.

  • Stronger C2-Additive form: $p = 2q + r$ with $q < p/2$, giving one canonical tree, no ± ambiguity.
  • Strictly stronger than Lemoine's conjecture (1895) on the primes: proving C2 gives Lemoine for primes as a corollary.
  • Verified for all 1,008,309,544 primes (up to ≈ 2·10¹⁰) with zero counterexamples, using a two-stage CUDA pipeline on an RTX 5070 at ≈150M primes/s.
  • Open question: does iterating $2q + r$ from $S_0=\{1,2\}$ generate exactly the primes? If so, it is a definition of primality that doesn't go through factorisation.

Paul Lockhart (A Mathematician's Lament) judged it “possibly even more difficult” than classical problems such as $p = n^2+1$.

Discrete Square Residual Structures (DSRS): every integer reveals its own connection to π

Zenodo · 10.5281/zenodo.17519713

Fix $\mu$ and set $U(n)=\lceil n^2/\mu\rceil$, $L(n)=\lfloor n^2/\mu\rfloor$. The infinite product

$$P(\mu)=\prod_{n=n_0}^{\infty}\frac{\Delta L(n)}{\Delta U(n)}$$

shows a sharp dichotomy (checked up to $N=10^6$, $\mu\le 70{,}000$): $P(\mu)\to\pi/2$ when $\mu\not\equiv 0 \pmod 4$, and $P(\mu)\to 1$ when $4\mid\mu$ or $\mu=1$.

  • Every non-multiple of 4 reduces to $\mu=2$ (a floor/ceiling encoding of the Wallis product) by residual cancellation.
  • OEIS correspondences, e.g. $\mu=2$: A052928, A007590, A000982, A063196; $\mu=4$: A008619, A002620, A004652, A161239.

Simon Plouffe called it “an interesting topic” and “not trivial”, forwarded it to Jean-Paul Allouche (CNRS) and offered an arXiv endorsement.

Fibonacci Numbers from Pascal Rows: A Ternary Coefficient Approach

Zenodo · 10.5281/zenodo.17412193

For which signs $c_i\in\{-1,0,1\}$ does $\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} c_i\binom{n-1}{i} = F_n$? A row of Pascal's triangle, a choice of add / subtract / skip for each entry, and a Fibonacci number as the target.

  • Six complete enumeration strategies with CUDA acceleration, exhaustive up to $3^{22}$ (31.4 billion) combinations.
  • A greedy $O(n)$ heuristic for the $O(3^n)$ space, built on palindromic symmetry and centre-dominance.
  • I have since found cases where the greedy rule fails; I'm now working out exactly when it holds.

Layered Pascal Transform Triangles: a unified framework for iterated finite differences on monomials

Zenodo · 10.5281/zenodo.18256084

Arrange the iterated forward differences $\Delta^{\ell}(x^p)$ as a family of triangles indexed by the layer $\ell$. Layer 0 is Pascal's triangle; higher layers keep a Pascal-like organisation.

  • A closed-form generator $C_{p,\ell}(v)$ produces single coefficients directly (no expansion, no Stirling-basis change), with $O(\ell)$ access when $p\gg\ell$.
  • Unifies five OEIS sequences (A259569, my own closed form, plus A001117, A000918, A000919, A000920) as fixed-layer projections of one structure.
  • Connects to Voigt (1901, Konformanten) and Piza (1947, D-coefficients), whose values appear in layers 2 and 3 but were never organised or unified.

Probability in Regular 2-Polytopes

Indian Mathematical Society Conference, MIT-WPU Pune · 25 December 2024

Drop a point (a 0-polytope) uniformly inside a regular polygon. Is it closer to the centroid or to the boundary? I proposed a periodic cotangent function to model how this probability depends on the polygon.

  • Independent work, 2022–2024; presented at the IMS conference with feedback from working mathematicians.
  • Written paper in progress; the full argument is in the talk video.

Dimensional Primality: A Geometric Hierarchy of Integers via Discrete Hyper-Rectangles

Draft on Zenodo · 10.5281/zenodo.20645595

Treat $N$ as the volume of a box whose integer sides are all greater than 1. The highest dimension in which $N$ can be built as such a genuine hyper-rectangle is exactly $\Omega(N)$, the number of its prime factors counted with multiplicity.

  • A strictly nested hierarchy $D_1 \supsetneq D_2 \supsetneq D_3 \supsetneq\cdots$ with $D_n=\{N:\Omega(N)\ge n\}$ and $\bigcap_n D_n=\varnothing$.
  • $n$-dimensional primes are the integers outside $D_n$: $1$ together with the $k$-almost primes for $k\le n-1$. The case $n=2$ gives back the ordinary primes.
  • Forced collapse: written in more than $\Omega(N)$ dimensions, at least $n-\Omega(N)$ sides must equal $1$.
  • The smallest member of $D_n$ is $2^n$, reached only by the cube $(2,\dots,2)$, the point where each new dimension first becomes possible.
